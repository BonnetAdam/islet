Everything in one island

All you expect from the notch. Then the parts nobody built.

Islet covers what the best notch apps do, drawn by the Mac's own graphics engine so it stays smooth and light. Then it opens the notch to your scripts and your agents.

Now playing

Cover, controls and a scrubber for Apple Music, Spotify and any player. The bars dance in the colour of the cover.

Volume and brightness

The keys show a quiet gauge in the notch instead of the big system square.

Battery and AirPods

Charging, low battery, and headphones or speakers the moment they connect.

File shelf and AirDrop

Drop files on the notch, carry them across Spaces, drag them out or send them with AirDrop.

Clipboard history

Your last copies, one click away. Kept in memory only, and passwords are never recorded.

Agenda

The next events of your day beside the clock, with a Join button for video calls.

Timer, colour picker, mirror

A countdown that lives in the notch, an eyedropper for any pixel, and a camera mirror before a call.

For developers

A notch your scripts can talk to.

Islet listens on a local socket that only your user account can reach. The islet command wraps it, so one line in any script puts a live activity beside the camera.

  • Progress rings, text, colours and SF Symbols
  • Claude Code hooks installed in one command
  • Allow or deny a tool from the notch, or answer in the terminal as usual
  • Links like islet://push for Shortcuts and apps

Questions

      
Does my Mac need a notch?
Islet is made for MacBooks with a notch. On other Macs and external displays it draws its own island at the top of the screen.

      
Is it really free?
Yes. Islet is free and open source under the MIT licence.

      
Which permissions does it ask for?
None to start. Accessibility is asked only if you want Islet to take over the volume and brightness keys, the calendar only when you tap to show your agenda, and the camera only when you open the mirror.

      
How does it read what is playing?
Through a small helper that asks macOS for the now playing information, the same way the system controls do. It works with any player that reports to macOS.

      
Can a website push things to my notch?
No. The socket lives in your user folder and only processes running as you can open it. Browsers cannot reach it.
